# Mehrab · مهراب

King of Kabul, father of Rudabeh. His descent from Zahhak does not mean he has snakes, demonic anatomy or Zahhak's costume. The poem describes his wisdom, strength and graceful bearing.

## Prompt line

Mehrab, the adult king of Kabul, tall and broad-shouldered at small manuscript scale, with an upright cypress-like bearing, an oval olive face, dark almond eyes, a straight nose and a neat short black beard. Black hair to his shoulders beneath a narrow gold diadem with one small malachite disc. A deep malachite-green long robe with an ivory collar, a narrow saffron sash, a plain gold belt and soft dark shoes. A short ceremonial straight sword in a closed dark scabbard, never drawn at a feast or audience. No snakes, no horns, no wings and no farr. He is dignified and recognisably human; all metal stays dimmer than the painting's central light.
